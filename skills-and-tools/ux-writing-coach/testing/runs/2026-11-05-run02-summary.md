## Cross-Scenario Findings

### What worked

- A1 demonstrated the intended plan-first workflow: a complete working plan, two clarifying questions, and no draft in the plan turn.
- A3 correctly skipped the plan because it was short, well-specified outage microcopy.
- Draft quality remained generally strong across service, evaluation, contact, boundary, and source-material tasks.
- A6 preserved the eligibility boundary and provided a thorough pre-publication checklist.
- A7 translated internal service language into user-facing tasks without republishing the definition.

### Where the plan-first workflow failed

- A2 skipped the plan even though the user supplied only source material and an output format.
- A4 combined the plan and recommendations in one turn.
- A5 logged `plan_stage: shown`, but the visible response contained a full section draft rather than a plan.
- A6 skipped the plan before giving feedback and revisions.
- A7 showed planning content, but did not use the standard compact plan pattern or end with an explicit review invitation.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. Tighten the plan-first rule so `input_type`, `copy_size`, and `molecule` are not enough to skip the plan.
2. Require the visible plan to use the five-field working-plan pattern before any recommendations or drafts.
3. Require the plan turn to end with an explicit “Reply with changes, or say ‘Go ahead’ to draft” invitation.
4. Add a consistency check between the logged `plan_stage` value and the visible response.
5. Rerun A2, A4, A5, A6, and A7 after the next skill update; use A1 and A3 as regression checks.
