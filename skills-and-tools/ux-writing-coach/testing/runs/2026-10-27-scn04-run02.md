### Turn 2 — Skill

> For a dual-audience prize page, use two parallel calls to action rather than one blended action:
>
> **Recommended**
> - Primary CTA: **Submit your work**
> - Secondary CTA: **Nominate a student project**
>
> This keeps the student action primary while making the faculty path visible without extra searching. The labels name both the action and object, which supports task-first microcopy, per [NN/g: Microcopy: Interaction Microcopy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-is-of-microcopy/#toc-interaction-microcopy-4).
>
> **Options**
> | Option | When to use | Tradeoff |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | **Submit your work** + **Nominate a student project** | Best default for separate submission and nomination paths | Requires two links, but is clearest |
> | **Start a submission** + **Start a nomination** | Better if the forms collect more than a final work, such as contact details or supporting materials | Slightly more neutral, but less concrete |
> | **Submit or nominate a project** | Use only if one form handles both roles | Shorter, but makes the reader determine the correct path |
>
> I would not make the five prize categories the primary CTA. Instead, place them in supporting copy or a secondary link such as **See the five prize categories**, then confirm the exact category names, especially whether “Special Collections” should be the full formal name. Once the deadline and submission URL are verified, the primary CTA can stay the same; the deadline should appear nearby in body copy, not inside the button label.
>
> Tell me whether students and faculty use separate forms, and I can refine the CTA hierarchy and surrounding copy.
