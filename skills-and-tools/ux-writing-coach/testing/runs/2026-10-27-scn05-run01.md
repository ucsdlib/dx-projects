## Full Transcript

### Turn 1 — User

> Our Ask page has a generic “Contact Us” tile, but most people need one of three things: directions or a known-item search, help with Library equipment or campus printing, or a referral for an in-depth research question. Please help me improve the help section.
>
> Available pathways are the Geisel Library Front Desk, WongAvery Front Desk, Library main telephone line, Contact Us form, and in-depth research consultations.

---

### Turn 1 — Skill

> Assumption: you want task-focused copy that can replace the generic tile and route people to the right pathway.
>
> **Recommended revision**
> Use one help section with three user tasks, then name the pathway after each task:
>
> - **Find an item or get directions**
>   Ask at the Geisel Library Front Desk or the WongAvery Front Desk.
>
> - **Get help with Library equipment or campus printing**
>   Contact the Library main telephone line or submit the Contact Us form.
>
> - **Get help with in-depth research**
>   Request an in-depth research consultation.
>
> This structure leads with what users need to do, then offers the most relevant channels. It also reduces the risk that “Contact Us” becomes a catch-all that hides the fastest route.
>
> **Bolder alternative**
> Consider changing the tile label from “Contact Us” to “Get help” or “Where to get help.” Then place the three tasks directly in the tile or on the linked page, rather than making users open a generic contact page first.
>
> Suggested tile copy:
>
> - **Find an item or get directions**
> - **Get equipment or printing help**
> - **Ask an in-depth research question**
>
> Next step: confirm which pathway you want people to use first for equipment and printing support, then have a Library content expert review the routing, locations, links, and hours before implementation.

---

### Turn 2 — User

> Use a task-specific contact and support structure. Lead with when to use each pathway and give the pathway name, but do not include hours or links because I have not verified them.

---

### Turn 2 — Skill

> **Recommended structure**
> Use this order:
>
> - Task: directions and known-item search
> - Task: Library equipment and campus printing
> - Task: in-depth research consultation
>
> **Draft copy**
> **Get help with Library questions**
>
> **Directions and known-item searches**
> Use the Geisel Library Front Desk if you need directions or help locating a known item in Geisel Library.
>
> Use the WongAvery Front Desk if you need directions or help locating a known item in WongAvery Library.
>
> **Library equipment and campus printing**
> Use the Library main telephone line if you need help with Library equipment or campus printing.
>
> Use the Contact Us form if you have questions about Library equipment or campus printing, or are not sure which pathway to use.
>
> **In-depth research consultations**
> Request an in-depth research consultation if you need sustained help with a research project, specialized sources, or a complex research question.
>
> **Bolder alternative**
> Replace “Contact Us” with “Get help.” Keep the three tasks visible in the tile or landing section, so users can choose the right pathway before opening the full page.
>
> Next step: confirm the equipment and printing pathway owners, then have a Library content expert review the routing, terminology, and accessibility of the final section.

---

### Turn 3 — User

> Would a single “Ask the Library” heading work better than three separate task headings?

---

### Turn 3 — Skill

> Yes, “Ask the Library” can work well as a single section heading. It is clearer than “Contact Us” because it signals help-seeking, not just messaging.
>
> I recommend using the single heading with task-led sentences beneath it. That preserves scanning without making the section feel fragmented:
>
> **Ask the Library**
>
> Use the Geisel Library Front Desk for directions or help locating a known item in Geisel Library.
>
> Use the WongAvery Front Desk for directions or help locating a known item in WongAvery Library.
>
> Use the Library main telephone line or Contact Us form for questions about Library equipment or campus printing.
>
> Request an in-depth research consultation for sustained help with a complex research project or specialized sources.
>
> Next step: have a Library content expert confirm the routing for equipment and printing and review the final terminology before publication.

## Evaluation Notes

### Strengths

- The task-specific structure is a strong replacement for the generic “Contact Us” tile.
- The single “Ask the Library” recommendation balances scanning and visual simplicity without hiding tasks.

### Issues and severity

- **Minor:** The third turn does not restate that hours and links remain unverified.
- **Minor:** The first draft asks the user to decide which pathway should come first for equipment and printing support, which is helpful but may require additional information from the service owner.
- **Minor:** The task headings are more direct in the two-heading structure than in the final single-heading structure, so usability testing would confirm whether any scanning benefit is lost.

## Improvement Ideas

1. Add a standing reminder for contact-and-support drafts to verify hours, links, response times, and preferred routing.
2. Add guidance for when a single section heading plus task-led sentences is preferable to task headings.
3. Recommend a compact mobile check for help pathways whenever multiple routes are provided.
