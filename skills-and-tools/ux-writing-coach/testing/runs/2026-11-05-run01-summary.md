## Cross-Scenario Findings

### What worked

- The V2 input-classification behavior held across both source material and rewrite-object scenarios.
- The working plan appeared when the structure or audience was unclear and did not block straightforward requests.
- All four scenarios used task-first copy and provided concrete verification or refinement next steps.
- A7 and A2 separated distinct user jobs instead of mapping source sentences directly into copy.
- A6 corrected the eligibility implication while preserving a welcoming tone.

### Issues

- Minor: Several responses reused the phrase “non-public or offsite locations,” which may be opaque to first-time users.
- Minor: Some citation labels were generic even when the underlying principle was more specific.
- Minor: A1 surfaced the internal five-minute expectation before the later turn confirmed whether it is safe to publish.
- Minor: A6’s alert led with the unavailable item before the available action.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. Add a light rule to translate storage or retrieval language into concrete user-facing wording when the source supports it.
2. Use more specific citation labels when the same source supports different principles.
3. Treat performance expectations as internal facts until the user confirms they are public commitments.
4. Lead availability alerts with what the user can do before naming the unavailable item.
