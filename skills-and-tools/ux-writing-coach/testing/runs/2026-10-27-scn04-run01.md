### Turn 1 — User

> Please evaluate this draft page section for the Undergraduate Library Research Prize. I am especially interested in whether it works for undergraduate students and faculty nominators.
>
> Current page:
>
> “Undergraduate Library Research Prize
>
> The UC San Diego Library is proud to announce the annual Undergraduate Library Research Prize, which recognizes outstanding work conducted by participating UC San Diego undergraduate students and how the Library contributed to their success. The prize helps demonstrate to the campus and broader community how the Library’s resources, services, and expertise are essential to effective research conducted by students at the undergraduate level. The Library currently offers 5 prizes. Work can be submitted if it was completed for class credit or presented at a research-focused event on campus. Submissions may be creative work, empirical research, or interpretive analysis. Students must be enrolled undergraduates in the Spring Quarter of the year in which they submit their work.”

---

### Turn 1 — Skill

> **Assumption:** This section sits near the top of the prize page and students are deciding whether to submit or nominate.
>
> **What Works**
> - The eligibility criteria are important and mostly clear.
> - Including creative work, empirical research, and interpretive analysis helps students see that many project types may qualify.
> - The page makes the Library’s role part of the prize story.
>
> **High-Impact Opportunities**
> - Lead with the student benefit and action instead of the Library’s announcement. Current phrasing centers institutional pride before telling students why the prize matters to them.
> - Clarify what “5 prizes” means. Are there five equal prizes, one per project type, separate award amounts, or something else? Without that detail, students cannot judge the opportunity accurately.
> - Add a direct path for faculty nominators. The draft does not say whether faculty nominate students, encourage submissions, or provide supporting materials, so that audience may not know what action to take.
>
> **Suggested Revision**
> The Undergraduate Library Research Prize recognizes outstanding UC San Diego undergraduate research and the role of Library resources, services, and expertise in that work.
>
> You may submit work completed for class credit or presented at a research-focused campus event. Projects may be creative work, empirical research, or interpretive analysis. The Library awards five prizes annually. To be eligible, you must be an enrolled undergraduate in Spring Quarter of the year you submit.
>
> Then add a clearly labeled section for eligibility, submission steps, and faculty nomination instructions. If faculty can nominate, name that action explicitly, for example: “Faculty can nominate a student project by submitting…” If students must self-submit, tell faculty how they can support or encourage students.
>
> **Next Step**
> Confirm the prize structure and submission or nomination process with the prize administrator, then have a few undergraduate students and faculty review the revised calls to action.

### Turn 2 — User

> What should the primary call to action be? We want students to submit their work and faculty to nominate student projects. There are five prize categories, including Special Collections and the Data & GIS Lab, but I have not confirmed the current deadline or submission link.

### Turn 2 — Skill

> **Recommended Primary CTA**
> Use two audience-specific calls to action, placed side by side near the top:
>
> - Students: **Submit your project**
> - Faculty: **Nominate a student project**
>
> **Options**
> - **Submit your project** — Best choice because it names the task concretely and works for creative, empirical, and interpretive work.
> - **Submit your work** — Also clear, but slightly less specific than “project.”
> - **Start your submission** — Useful if the submission requires multiple steps, but it may feel less direct.
>
> **Supporting Copy**
> Under the buttons, use brief text such as:
>
> Choose from five prize categories, including Special Collections and the Data & GIS Lab.
>
> Then list all five categories once confirmed. Avoid “Get started” or a single “Learn more” button because it does not distinguish the two audiences or their actions.
>
> **Important**
> Do not publish a deadline or submission link until the current information is confirmed. Use a visible placeholder such as “Deadline: coming soon” only if the page must launch before that information is available, then replace it as soon as the prize administrator confirms the details.
>
> One question that may affect the design: do student submissions and faculty nominations go through the same form or separate processes?

### Turn 3 — User

> Give me one revised opening paragraph that handles both audiences without becoming too long.

### Turn 3 — Skill

> **Revised Opening**
> The Undergraduate Library Research Prize recognizes outstanding UC San Diego undergraduate research and the Library resources, services, and expertise that support it. Undergraduate students may submit creative work, empirical research, or interpretive analysis completed for class credit or presented at a research-focused campus event. Faculty may nominate student projects. The Library awards five prizes, including categories from Special Collections and the Data & GIS Lab, to students enrolled during Spring Quarter of the submission year.
>
> This version leads with the prize and its benefit, gives each audience a clear action, and keeps eligibility at the end. Before publication, have the prize administrator verify the category names and enrollment requirement.
