### Turn 3 — User

> Someone reviewing the draft asked whether we should show the under-five-minute expectation and the out-of-scope list. What would you recommend?

---

### Turn 3 — Skill

> **Recommendation**
> - Do not publish “under five minutes.” It is an internal workflow expectation, and a specific number can become a promise the service cannot always meet.
> - Use a broader user-centered cue instead: “Get quick answers” or “Ask staff for quick help.” This communicates speed without setting a rigid time expectation.
> - Do not publish the full out-of-scope list as a negative block. Include only the distinctions most likely to prevent confusion, framed around what happens next.
> - I would add a short limits-and-referrals passage such as:  
>   “For in-depth research questions, topic development, or personalized research support, staff can refer you to the right service or specialist.”
>
> If user questions or service data show frequent confusion about personal technology support, add that boundary as well. A Library content expert should confirm the scope language, and user testing would help determine whether visitors understand the difference between quick help and research support.
