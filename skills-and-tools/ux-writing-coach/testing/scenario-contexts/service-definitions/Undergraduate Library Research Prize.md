## Description

Start with a brief description of the service, introducing its significance for UC San Diego. (This part is typically written last). 


The Undergraduate Library Research Prize recognizes excellence in the strategic and effective use of the UC San Diego Library as a resource in direct support of research undertaken on campus by students at the undergraduate level.

## Service Vision

Articulate the forward-facing vision for this service - where are we now (what is the purpose of the service) and where are we headed next? 


The prize recognizes the outstanding work conducted by participating UC San Diego undergraduate students and how the Library contributed to their success. The prize helps demonstrate to the campus and broader community how the Library’s resources, services, and expertise are essential to effective research conducted by students at the undergraduate level. 


The Library currently offers 5 prizes: Best Project in Life & Health Sciences; Best Project in Physical Sciences & Engineering; Best Project in Social Sciences, Arts & Humanities; Best Project Utilizing the UC San Diego Special Collections; and Best Projects Utilizing the Data & GIS Lab.  


In the ideal future, the Library will work with a donor to name the prize and thus sustain it through a reliable endowment. Depending on available funding, the Library can increase or decrease the number of categories and prizes given.

## User Goals and Jobs to be Done (optional)

### User Goals

The high-level outcome users seek to achieve in their lives, education, or work. Example: “Develop teaching materials.” User goals are often bigger than the service itself. The service helps users make progress toward achieving their goals.

- To demonstrate sophistication, originality, and/or unusual depth or breadth in researching Library collections, including, but not limited to, printed resources, databases, datasets, maps, primary resources, and materials in all media
- To demonstrate exceptional ability to locate, select, evaluate, and synthesize Library resources to create a project in any media that shows originality and/or has the potential to lead to original research
- To demonstrate evidence of significant personal learning with regard to the practice of research and inquiry that shows a likelihood of persisting in the future
- To demonstrate evidence of understanding how research practices (such as topic selection, resource selection, and research methods) intersect with the furthering of equity, diversity, and inclusion

## Measures of Success

How do we know the service is successful? List the measures of success here. Includes the measure, how it will be measured, and how often it will be measured. 

| **Measure** | **Process** |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
|  |  |

Metrics used to evaluate Undergraduate Library Research Prize submissions is reflected upon each year at a project debrief meeting: 

1. The Library evaluates promotion of the prize by reviewing the number of submissions received; prizes receiving fewer submissions in a given year may become areas of focus for the coordinator in subsequent years
2. Submissions are evaluated by a rubric created by project judges; the rubric is evaluated annually by the judges to ensure that it is timely and reflects the goals of the prize 
3. The measure of success is seeing that the rubric-aligned scores of the submitted work reflect a level of research mastery worthy of receiving the prize; while scores might vary year to year, judges ensure that submitted work meets their expectations and is deserving of the prize
