# Borrow and Use Physical Collections Service Definition

## Description

The Borrow and Use Physical Collections service provides access to the UC San Diego Library’s onsite print and media collections for in-person use and loan, supporting users in requesting, obtaining, and using materials from the Library’s general and select mediated collections. Through a mix of self-service options and staff support, users can locate items in the stacks, request retrieval from non-public or offsite locations, borrow eligible items for offsite use, and use non-circulating materials in Library spaces, with clear expectations and consistent processes that make access predictable, equitable, and transparent.

## Service Vision

Users can confidently rely on timely, equitable, and predictable access to physical print and media collections, regardless of their familiarity with the Library. It aims to make it easy to understand what can be borrowed, for how long, and how to get help; to provide clear, consistent options for both self-service and staff‑mediated access; to offer transparent communication and turnaround times for requests; and to reduce barriers so that physical collections are responsibly stewarded and remain usable for current and future teaching, learning, and research needs.

## Service Scope (Boundaries)

Articulate the parameters of the service. What is in scope? What is out of scope? 

| **In Scope ** |
| --- |
| Borrowing of physical items in UC San Diego collections, including technology |
| Borrowing of circulating print materials including items obtained through resource sharing services |
| Access to non-circulating materials |
| Onsite playback of library owned media |
| Library account management, including registration and renewal for community borrowers |
| Maintanence and assistance with self-service holdshelves |

| **Out of Scope** |
| --- |
| Access to Special Collections & Archives Collection materials |
| Assistance with viewing or playback of personal media |
| Reformatting support or services |
| Immediate retrieval of materials in the general collections |

## Who Uses This Service and Why?

List and define user groups and communities (e.g. researchers, learners, instructors, etc.). If possible, list the highest priority audiences and user groups first. \[Note the difference between “user groups” and “affiliations” - affiliations like “faculty” “student”, etc are distinct from user groups. This is important because each affiliation type might be associated with multiple user groups (a faculty member can be a researcher and an instructor depending on the purpose and the need.

While access to onsite collections is available to all visitors of the Library, regardless of affiliation, the ability to borrow materials for offsite use is limited to University affiliates and registered Community Borrowers. Use of restricted items (Library Use Only) and media by non-cardholders does require abbreviated registration to assist with the tracking of materials within the building.

| **User Groups** | **What are they trying to accomplish?** |
| --- | --- |
| Borrowers | 1. **Rapid Check‑Out/In Assistance:** Front‑desk staff provide quick, error‑free checkout and self‑checkout kiosk help, minimizing wait time so borrowers can get their materials and return them on schedule. 2. **Renewal & Hold Support:** Immediate processing of renewals and placement of holds, with real‑time status updates and email notifications so borrowers never lose a needed item. 3. **Fine & Fee Guidance:** Clear, friendly explanations of overdue policies, fine waivers, and payment options, helping borrowers stay in good standing and encouraging continued use of library resources. |
| Listeners / Viewers | 1. **Access to library media collections** 2. **Playback support: **Access and introduction to specialized equipment for media playback. |

## What Demonstrates Success for This Service?

How do we know the service is successful? These could be either quantitative or qualitative metrics. Consider how often these metrics should be measured and reviewed.

- **Positive Impressions** – 90 %+ of users feel “welcome and well‑served” in post‑visit surveys.
- **Efficient Service** – 90% of routine checkouts are resolved at the desk; any escalated issues receive follow up within 2 business days.
- **Efficient Self-Service **– 75% of attempts to use self-service options are successful // Self-service transactions represent at least 20% of total checkouts
- **Use of Media collections** – 90% of non-Special Collections Library media collections are accessible/usable onsite.

## Service Calendar/Service Workflows

- Annual
    - Review of statistics to inform coverage needs and update scheduling templates
    - Identification of [Contributing Librarians](https://ucsdlibrary.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/UX/pages/3474718759/Front+Desk+Contributing+Librarians)
    - Comprehensive review of [Front Desk Service Manual](http://ucsd.libguides.com/slaservicedesk)
    - [User Account Purge](https://ucsdlibrary.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ils/pages/1604419585/Purging+User+Records+in+Alma) (January and July)
- Quarterly
    - Creation of persistent schedule for academic quarter/summer
- Monthly
    - Adjustments to service schedule for planned absences
- Weekly
    - Inventory of self-service holdshelf and follow up regarding missing items
    - [Patron Download](https://ucsdlibrary.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ils/pages/2062778515/SIS+Integration+Setup+Patron+Load+with+Alma)
- Daily
    - Review and clearing of 2-Day holdshelf
    - Clearing of expired holds
- As Needed
    - Adjustments to service schedule for unplanned absences
