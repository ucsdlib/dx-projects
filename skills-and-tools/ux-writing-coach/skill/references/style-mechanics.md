# Style Mechanics

Use this reference for UC San Diego naming, capitalization, numbers, dates and times, contact details, punctuation, links, and common web or interface terms. For source precedence and uncertainty, see [library-style.md](library-style.md).

## UC San Diego Naming

- Use **UC San Diego**, never “UCSD.” For the most formal first reference, use **University of California San Diego** with no comma between “University” and “San Diego.”
- Use **UC San Diego Library** whenever possible. On later reference, “the Library” is acceptable after the full name has appeared. Never use “UCSD Library.”
- Prefer **campus** when referring to UC San Diego as a place or institution. Lowercase “campus” and “university” when used alone.
- For UC campuses, use standard names such as UC Berkeley, UC Davis, UC Irvine, UCLA, UC Merced, UC Riverside, UCSF, UC Santa Barbara, and UC Santa Cruz. Use “the 10-campus UC System” when clarification is needed.
- Capitalize proper nouns and formal titles before names. Lowercase generic terms such as “database,” “research guide,” “college,” “department,” and “library” unless they are part of a formal name.
- Capitalize “Library” or “the Library” when referring to UC San Diego Library. Do not capitalize generic “library.”

## Capitalization

- Follow AP and avoid unnecessary capitalization.
- Use sentence case for web headings: capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.
- Lowercase occupational descriptions and titles after a name: “history professor Ruth Rosen,” “Esperanza Nee, director of financial aid.”
- Capitalize formal titles only when they precede a name: “Chancellor Pradeep K. Khosla,” but “the chancellor.”
- Lowercase offices, departments, centers, committees, programs, majors, and disciplines unless the exact proper name requires capitalization.
- Do not capitalize the part of a hyphenated word that follows a prefix when the prefix is not a standalone word: “Co-founder,” “Well-Being.”
- In plural uses, lowercase common-noun elements of names: “the Democratic and Republican parties,” “Main and State streets.”

## Numbers and Percentages

- Spell out one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above.
- Use numerals with units of measure, ages, dimensions, distances, monetary units, sequential designations, speeds, temperatures, and times.
- Spell out ordinals first through ninth. Use figures for 10th and above.
- Use the percent symbol with a numeral: “50%.” Spell out with zero: “zero percent.”
- For percentage ranges, use “12%-15%,” “12% to 15%,” or “between 12% and 15%.”
- In tables, forms, and statistical or sequential content, prefer numerals for consistency and scannability.

## Dates, Times, and Time Zones

- Spell out one through nine and use numerals for 10 and above in dates when a specific date is given.
- Abbreviate months with six or more letters when used with a specific date: “Jan. 15.” Spell out March, April, May, June, and July.
- Do not use ordinal suffixes with numerals in dates: “Jan. 15,” not “Jan. 15th.”
- Use “8 a.m.–5 p.m.,” not “8:00 AM to 5:00 PM.” Lowercase “a.m.” and “p.m.” and use periods.
- Use “noon” and “midnight” rather than “12 p.m.” and “12 a.m.” Use a colon to separate hours from minutes.
- Use a hyphen or “to” for time ranges: “9-11 a.m.,” “9 a.m. to 5 p.m.”
- Include a time zone when the audience spans zones. Prefer “6 p.m. Pacific Standard Time” or “6 p.m. Pacific Daylight Time” over abbreviations when possible.
- For academic-year ranges, do not repeat the first two digits when the century stays the same: “2009-10.” Use “1999-2000” when it does.

## Addresses, Phone, and Email

- Spell out street suffixes such as “Avenue,” “Boulevard,” and “Street” when no specific address is given. Use AP abbreviations when a full address is supplied.
- Format domestic phone numbers with hyphens, not parentheses: “212-621-1500.” Use “(toll free)” before a toll-free number if needed.
- Format international numbers with country and city codes: “011-44-20-7535-1515.”
- Show extensions with a comma: “212-621-1500, ext. 2.”
- Lowercase email addresses. Capitalize proper nouns and acronyms in email subject lines; otherwise use sentence case.

## Web and Interface Terms

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| email | e-mail |
| homepage | home page |
| internet, intranet | Internet, Intranet |
| website, web page | Website, webpage |
| Wi-Fi | wifi, Wifi |
| log in, log on, log off as verbs | login as a verb |
| login, logon, logoff as nouns | log in as a noun |
| sign in where the product uses that term | inconsistent sign in / log in within one interface |

## Abbreviations and Acronyms

- Avoid unnecessary abbreviations and acronyms in user-facing copy.
- If an acronym is necessary, spell out the full name on first reference and place the acronym in parentheses: “Interlibrary Loan (ILL).”
- Generally omit periods in acronyms unless omitting them would create an unrelated word.
- Follow the source’s preferred abbreviation for a formal name. If the name is not in the reference files, verify it rather than inventing an abbreviation.

## Punctuation and Lists

- Avoid em dashes. Use commas, periods, colons, or semicolons instead.
- In a simple series, omit the Oxford comma unless needed to prevent misreading.
- Capitalize the first word after a colon only when it is a proper noun or begins a complete sentence.
- Use a semicolon to separate complex list items that already contain commas.
- Use curly or smart quotes and apostrophes in display copy. Use straight quotes only when the interface requires them.
- Capitalize the first word of each numbered or bulleted item. Do not add final punctuation unless the items are full sentences.
- Use parallel construction for list items: same part of speech, voice, tense, and sentence type.

## Links and URLs

- Make link text specific and descriptive. Avoid “click here,” “read more,” bare URLs, and generic “learn more” without an object.
- Omit `http://`, `https://`, and `www.` from visible URLs when the shortened form still works.
- Lowercase visible domain names for standard practice and accessibility. Keep the exact case of a page path when case sensitivity matters.
- Do not link the final period when a URL ends a sentence in body copy. In display copy, the final period may be removed for visual clarity.
- Use short links or vanity URLs for long internal URLs when appropriate. Prefer dashes over underscores in custom URLs.

## Titles of Works, Courses, and Events

- Capitalize and use no quotes for concerts and events: “Library Research Week.”
- Put lecture titles and titles of works in quotation marks: “Finding Primary Sources.”
- Capitalize course numbers and formal course names as the campus catalog presents them. Lowercase “course,” “class,” and “workshop” when generic.
- Capitalize “Week” and use a numeral when referring to a specific week of the quarter: “Week 2.” Lowercase “first week of winter quarter.”

## Accessibility-Adjacent Copy

- Make link text specific enough to be understood out of context.
- Make interface labels understandable without surrounding text.
- Use sentence case for headings and navigation.
- Avoid image-only meaning when the same message can be conveyed in text.
- Refer full accessibility audits and conformance questions to `ucsd-accessibility-compliance`.
