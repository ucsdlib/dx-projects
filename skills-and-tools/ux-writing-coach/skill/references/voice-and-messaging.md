# Voice and Messaging

Use this reference to tune tone, voice, headlines, body copy, promotional messaging, and service-critical language. For source precedence, see [library-style.md](library-style.md).

## UC San Diego Voice

The UC San Diego compass is **“Curiosity Must.”** It is internal, but it should shape the feel of outward-facing copy. Ask whether the copy feels curious and driven.

Keep five tone qualities present, with emphasis adjusted to context:

- **Courageous:** confident without being institutional.
- **Sincere:** honest and human, never performative.
- **Consequential:** connected to something real.
- **Agile:** forward-moving without bureaucratic drag.
- **Collective:** community-centered.

Use “you” and “we.” Use contractions where they sound natural. Take clear positions while staying professional.

## Library Voice

Library copy should feel like UC San Diego and sound like the Library. The Library is welcoming, inclusive, and helpful.

- **Warm:** Write to a person, not a user profile. Use “you” and “we,” contractions where natural, and approachable language.
- **Inviting:** Every page is an open door. Lead with what users can do before listing requirements or restrictions.
- **Inclusive:** Assume nothing about prior experience, confidence, or familiarity with Library systems. A first-year student and an experienced researcher may use the same page.
- **Curious:** Signal that questions, incomplete research plans, and not knowing where to start are welcome.

Ask: Does this feel inclusive? Does it feel helpful? Would a student who has never used an academic library feel welcomed or overwhelmed?

## Messaging Themes

Use these themes as lenses, not slogans. Pair them with the user’s task and concrete Library value:

- chasing and finding comfort in the unknown;
- embracing curiosity;
- pursuing knowledge and understanding;
- engaging local and worldly cultures and San Diego’s heritage;
- celebrating agile, open-minded work;
- thinking further, even from a familiar place;
- getting out into the world and beyond comfort zones.

## Headlines

Headlines should be succinct, bold, thought-provoking, and on brand. They may be complete thoughts, questions, or thought starters that the supporting copy expands.

Possible approaches:

- Ask a provocative question.
- Make a bold statement or reveal a little-known fact.
- Pair a scientific or factual idea with an emotional mindset.
- Juxtapose large ideas with intimate moments.
- Question the impossible or explore a “what if.”
- Let the featured subject’s inner thought speak.

For Library UX, still make the headline meaningful. A clever line must not force users to guess the page’s task.

## Body Copy

Body copy should be to the point, engaging, and digestible. Open with a hook when the content is promotional or explanatory, then answer or elaborate quickly.

Messaging best practices:

- **Grab attention and hold it.** Use a provocative question, captivating insight, or clever phrasing, then follow with substance.
- **Sound human.** Be relatable, conversational, and excitable. Avoid clichés, marketing jargon, and overly verbose or lofty language.
- **Prefer less.** Keep headlines bite-sized and body copy easy to read. Reword, reorder, or find a clearer way to say the same idea.
- **Stay curious.** Invite users into the conversation. Open-ended questions can work when they do not obscure the task.
- **Be bright.** Convey optimism and joy, but keep pride and professionalism. Avoid humor that becomes whimsical or distracting.

## Tone by Context

| Context | Tone priority | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service page | Clear, warm, action-first | Lead with what the user can do; explain requirements after the path. |
| Policy or eligibility | Clear, respectful, honest | State the allowed action first; preserve limits plainly. |
| Error or outage | Calm, concrete, reassuring | Say what happened, what to do next, and where to get help. |
| Access restriction | Helpful, specific | Name the door and the credential or condition, not just the wall. |
| Event copy | Bright, specific, inviting | Include date, time, location, audience, and action. |
| Promotional copy | Curious, human, energetic | Avoid hype, clichés, and unsupported claims. |
| Research support | Credible, curious, precise | Use plain language for complex concepts without oversimplifying. |

## Service-Critical Copy

For errors, outages, access restrictions, policies, and emergency information, clarity and reassurance come before brand flair. Say:

1. what happened;
2. why it may have occurred, if useful;
3. what the user can do next;
4. whether an alternative exists;
5. where to get help.

Do not hide material limits, imply eligibility that does not exist, or invent alternatives. Boundaries can be respectful and clear.

## AI-Sounding Language

Avoid em dashes, hype, throat-clearing, vague intensifiers, and algorithmic patterns. See [library-style.md](library-style.md) for the full list and replacements.
