# UC San Diego Library Editorial Style Guide

This Library Editorial Style Guide — like the [University Communications Editorial Style Guide](https://univcomms.ucsd.edu/resources/editorial-style-guide/index.html) and the [UC San Diego Web Style Guide](https://blink.ucsd.edu/technology/websites/training/writing/styleguide/index.html) — generally follows [The Associated Press (AP) Stylebook](http://www.apstylebook.com/). 

University Communications strongly encourages departments to follow AP style in all public communications, including websites, print and electronic brochures/promotional materials, newsletters, etc., to ensure consistency throughout UC San Diego communications.

The Library offers [subscription access to the online AP Stylebook](https://search-library.ucsd.edu/discovery/fulldisplay?docid=alma991009762279706535&context=L&vid=01UCS_SDI:UCSD&lang=en&search_scope=ArticlesBooksEtc&adaptor=Local%20Search%20Engine&tab=ArticlesBooksEtc&query=any,contains,ap%20style%20guide&offset=0) to users on UC San Diego IP addresses (on network or VPN). To ensure availability for others, don't leave the website open longer than needed.

## **UC and UC San Diego Terminology**

**campuswide, campus-wide**

One word or with a hyphen is acceptable; capitalized only at the beginning of sentences

**systemwide, system-wide **

- One word or with a hyphen is acceptable for the University of California campuses and labs
- Systemwide is also used for LAUC (e.g. LAUC-Systemwide, rather than LAUC-Statewide)

**universitywide, university-wide**

One word or with a hyphen is acceptable; capitalized only at the beginning of sentences; interchangeable with "systemwide" to refer to the campuses in the UC System

**University of California**

All of the following are acceptable:

- University of California
- the university (always lowercase unless at the beginning of a sentence)
- UC
- UC System (always capitalize “System” when used with “UC”)

If confusion with UC San Diego is likely, refer to "the 10-campus UC System." University of California Office of the President may be abbreviated as "UC Office of the President" or "UCOP." Do not use "Systemwide" as a title for UCOP. However, "systemwide" is acceptable as an adjective. Example:

- The committee reviewed systemwide personnel policies.

Abbreviate other UC campuses as UC Berkeley, UC Davis, UC Irvine, UCLA, UC Merced, UC Riverside, UCSF, UC Santa Barbara, and UC Santa Cruz.

**UC San Diego**

[Per campus brand guidelines](https://ucpa.ucsd.edu/brand/story/use-of-the-university-name/), please follow this protocol in all communications:

- First reference, spell out the name: University of California San Diego (no comma between "University" and "San Diego"); this is most suitable for formal communications like a press release, or correspondence on university letterhead
- Subsequent references and in headlines, use the abbreviated version of the name: UC San Diego (not UCSD)
- You can also use other words such as “campus” or “institution” to refer to UC San Diego in various communications efforts.

## **UC San Diego Library**

UC San Diego Library should be spelled out whenever possible; use “the Library” in subsequent references after the full name is spelled out. “The,” as in “the UC San Diego Library” should only be capitalized at the beginning of sentences. Per desired campus branding, never use “UCSD Library.”

## **Library Program and Unit Names**

All names should be spelled out in the first instance, with the acronym in parentheses directly following, and can be abbreviated in subsequent references. Ampersands should be used when the full name is spelled out and can be used (or not) with abbreviated forms of the name. The word “Program” can be used as a formal part of the name, if desired.

## **Library Names and Buildings**

- **Geisel Library** should be used when referring to the library itself; **Geisel Library building** can be used in references to the structure; Geisel or Geisel Library are acceptable uses after the full name is spelled out
- **WongAvery Library **should be used when referring to the library itself; **WongAvery Library building** can be used in references to the structure; WongAvery or WongAvery Library are acceptable uses after the full name is spelled out; WongAvery is one word, no hyphen or space, with a capital “W” and a capital “A;” WongAvery should not be abbreviated, e.g., WAL.

## **General Library Terminology**

- **Get Help**, not “LibAnswers” or “Ask A Librarian”
- **Borrower Definitions**
    - **Community borrower** – a member of the local community who is a UC San Diego Library cardholder with borrowing privileges
    - **Member of the public **– member of the local community who does not (yet!) have a UC San Diego Library card
- **Cardholder**, not “card holder” 
- **Checkout period**, not “loan” or “borrowing period”; only capitalize at the beginning of a sentence
- **Course reserves**; capitalize only at the beginning of a sentence; “reserves” can be used after the full name is spelled out
- **Current journals**, not “unbound journals” or “periodicals”; capitalize only at the beginning of a sentence
- **E-book**; capitalize the “E” only when used at the beginning of a sentence
- **E-journals**; capitalize the “E” only when used at the beginning of a sentence
- **E-reserves**; use the full name “electronic reserves” in the first instance; “e-reserves” can be used subsequently; only capitalize “E” when used at the beginning of a sentence; “R” should not be capitalized
- **Explore magazine**, not “EXPLORE”or “*Explore*”; always capitalize the “E” but do not place it in quotes or italicize; lowercase “magazine” as it is not part of the publication's formal title
- **Full-text**, not “fulltext;” capitalize only at the beginning of a sentence
- **Grab and Go whiteboard supplies**
- **Interlibrary Loan**; “Interlibrary”is always one word and both words should be capitalized; the full name should be written out first; “ILL” can be used subsequently
- **Library Guides **or **LibGuides**; refers to software familiar only to librarians and Library staff, therefore, as a first reference in public documents use the name of the specific guide you are referring to (e.g., U.S. Government Library Guide), or “Library Guides.” As a secondary reference, use “Library Guide” or “LibGuide”
- **Librarywide, Library-wide**; one word or with a hyphen is acceptable
- **Limited use**, not “controlled circulation” or “ConCirc;” only capitalize at the beginning of a sentence (formerly “Library Use Only”)
- **Local Scanning Service**; name of the service of scanning and electronically delivering scans of UC San Diego-owned collection materials; not document delivery or digital delivery-do not used; service discontinued in Summer 2025 due to reorganization
- **Monographs**; avoid using this term in public references, except in Library statistics; use “books” instead, which is readily understood by non-library audiences
- **Open Access**; capitalize only at the beginning of a sentence or when it is being used as part of a name; “OA” can be used in subsequent references
- **Peer-reviewed** and **peer review process**; capitalize only at the beginning of a sentence; hyphenate when used as an adjective
- **Pickup **(n. and adj.), **pick up** (v.)
    - Curbside **Pickup**, as a service
    - User will **pick up** a book, as an action
    - Never **pick-up **with a hyphen
- **Serials**; avoid using this term in public references, except in Library statistics; use “periodicals” or “journals” instead, as they are readily understood by non-library audiences

## **Frequently Used Electronic Terms**

- **Email**; one word, no hyphen; lowercase “e” except at the beginning of a sentence; use a hyphen with other e- terms: e-book, e-reader, e-commerce
- **Homepage**; one word; lowercase “h” except at the beginning of a sentence
- **Internet**; lowercase “I” except at the beginning of a sentence
- **Intranet**; lowercase “I” except at the beginning of a sentence
- **Public Computers**
- **SSO Computers**
- **Website**; one word and only capitalized at the beginning of a sentence; “web page” is two words with no hyphen and only capitalized at the beginning of a sentence
- **Wi-Fi**, not “wifi” or “Wifi”
