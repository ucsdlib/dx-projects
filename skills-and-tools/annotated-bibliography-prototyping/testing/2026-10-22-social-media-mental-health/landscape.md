## Summary

**Research question:** How does social media affect mental health among college students, especially through anxiety, depression, sleep, and social comparison?

**Context:** Psychology 190 capstone prep; 8-10 scholarly, preferably peer-reviewed sources in APA style; ages 18-24; no news articles; recent sources preferred.

### Search strategy

UC Library Search was used first through its Primo PNX fallback. Three focused queries returned 20 records each; after de-duplication, 53 records were screened and 12 were selected for the landscape. A 2016 sleep source was retained as a direct-fit exception to the preferred 2018-2026 range.

## Overviews

The results include three useful reviews and a meta-analysis:

- [The Use of Social Networking Sites in Mental Health Interventions for Young People: Systematic Review, Ridout & Campbell (2018)](https://doi.org/10.2196/12244) - A systematic review of SNS-based youth mental-health interventions.
- [Unifying the detrimental and beneficial effects of social network site use on self-esteem, Krause et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2019.1656646) - A systematic review organizing SNS effects on self-esteem through comparison, feedback, and self-reflection.
- [Relationships between social networking sites use and subjective well-being, Yang & Feng (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e32463) - A meta-analysis and structural equation model across 73 independent samples.

## Themes

These names describe the retrieved landscape rather than conclusions about the whole field.

1. **Problematic use and mental health:** Retrieved sources discuss associations between excessive or problematic SNS use and mental-health symptoms.
2. **Social comparison, envy, and self-esteem:** Retrieved sources link comparison and envy on SNSs to self-esteem, depression, and anxiety.
3. **Sleep quality and timing:** Retrieved sources connect social-media timing, fatigue, problematic use, and sleep quality among students.
4. **Interventions and limiting use:** Retrieved sources evaluate limiting use or using SNS platforms to deliver mental-health support.
5. **Social support and compensatory use:** Retrieved sources discuss family support, peer support, and SNS use as compensation during college transition.
6. **Peer disclosure and stigma:** One retrieved source examines how students recognize and respond to peers' mental-health disclosures on SNSs.

## Possible directions

One proposed direction would be to compare activity type rather than only total time: whether passive scrolling, upward comparison, positive feedback, or self-reflection have different associations. This is proposed based on measurement differences noted in the retrieved reviews, not a claim that the literature itself asks this exact question.
