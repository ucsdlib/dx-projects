# Undergraduate Capstone Landscape

## Summary

**Research question:** What factors help undergraduate capstone projects and courses succeed?

**Context:** This test focuses on undergraduate research capstones across disciplines, with emphasis on project design, student experience, engagement, and professional preparation. The working scope is 2016–2026 and includes scholarly articles, conference proceedings, and books.

### Search strategy

I ran a focused UC Library Search landscape, then one component search on course and project design. The two API searches screened 26 records; after duplicate removal, 22 unique records were classified and 11 were retained for the landscape. The searches surfaced enough coherent themes to stop for orientation, but this is not an exhaustive survey.

## Themes

The retrieved landscape is concentrated in how capstone experiences are designed, experienced, and connected to professional preparation.

1. **Scaffolded design thinking:** One multi-iteration study treats design thinking as a staged process for collaborative capstone work.
2. **Student perceptions of project-based capstones:** Studies examine what students find valuable or challenging in project-based and flipped capstones.
3. **Stakeholder alignment and incentives:** One engineering-education source models partially aligned objectives among students, faculty, and sponsors.
4. **Equity and engagement in applied projects:** One IoT-capstone study examines engagement for historically underrepresented students.
5. **Course design models and preparation:** Several sources describe course structures and design principles intended to prepare students for professional practice.
6. **Career information literacy:** One study compares student and employer emphases on career information literacy in capstone units.

## Possible directions

1. **How do capstone teams adjust project scope when resources, sponsor expectations, or deadlines conflict?** This is a proposed direction based on the stakeholder-incentive source and course-design sources, not a source-backed claim.
2. **Which course-design elements transfer best across disciplinary capstones?** This is a proposed direction based on the variety of disciplinary cases retrieved, not a finding that transfer has been established.
