# Novice Visualization Review

**Reviewer role:** novice city staff member or interested resident  
**Inputs used:** case brief, Mermaid map, force-directed HTML view, and linear reading view  
**Difficulty:** 3 out of 5

## What I could understand

The main research question is clear: what evidence and governance actions should a novice city stakeholder consider when addressing extreme heat and public health? It is broad, but the map successfully breaks it into useful themes rather than presenting the prompt as a research conclusion.

The main themes are:

1. Health outcomes and healthcare use
2. Vulnerability, equity, and access
3. Urban form, green space, and cooling
4. Governance, warnings, and emergency response
5. Lived experience and social consequences
6. Measurement and evidence limitations

I could find a source supporting a specific question. Iungman et al. (2023), “Cooling cities through urban green infrastructure: A health impact assessment of European cities,” directly supports the question, “How do urban form and green infrastructure influence cooling and heat-related health outcomes?”

The stated-versus-inferred distinction is visible on source cards. Most of the sampled source questions were labeled “Inferred.” That labeling is useful for a novice, although I initially expected “direct support” to mean that the source explicitly asked the same question in full text.

The distinction between direct support and cross-cutting relevance is understandable once I look at the links. For example, Iungman et al. directly supports the urban-form/green-infrastructure question. By contrast, the health-outcomes theme is shown as cross-cutting to that question because health is related, not because that source directly measures health outcomes.

## Relationship filter

In the interactive HTML view, I would use the “Relationships” control to isolate link types:

- **Direct support** to see only evidence connections between questions and sources.
- **Secondary links** to see conceptual relationships that are not counted as direct support.
- **Cross-cutting links** to see how a theme applies across more than one question.
- **Possible next questions** to see future inquiry suggestions separately from current evidence.

The filter is helpful, but “secondary” versus “cross-cutting” takes a moment to interpret. A short inline explanation in the view would make this easier.

## One possible next question

I would start with: “How should cooling-center placement be evaluated against uptake, lived experience, and equity?” This feels practical for a city and connects the existing access evidence to equity and lived experience.

## What I would do next

I would pick the cooling-center question, refine it for our city, and ask for a focused search on accessibility, actual use, and resident experiences. I would also look separately at the city-specific heat-health action-plan question because the map says city-specific causal evaluations were not found in the retrieved coverage.

## Confusing or misleading points

- The Mermaid diagram is difficult to read because source cards contain long citations and question text. The linear view solves this problem much better.
- Calling an edge “direct support” while also labeling the source question “Inferred” and noting metadata-only evidence could confuse a novice. The map does explain this limitation, but the wording on the diagram itself may still suggest stronger evidence than intended.
- The same core questions repeat under multiple themes. This is appropriate for cross-cutting topics, but it makes the map feel like it contains more distinct questions than the stated seven.
- The equity theme includes sources about measurement, access, lived experience, and emergency communication. That is useful, but the broad scope may make it less clear which evidence directly addresses equity rather than merely being relevant to it.

## Verdict

**Conditional pass.** A novice can identify the main question, themes, source-question distinction, relationship types, and a practical next question. However, the Mermaid view is visually overloaded, and the labels for “direct support,” “inferred,” and “metadata only” could mislead someone into treating title-based relevance as stronger evidence than it is.
