## Coverage summary

- **Strong confirmed-scope coverage:** The pool covers health outcomes and care use, vulnerable populations/equity, urban greening and cooling, governance/policy/emergency response, resident experience, and evidence limitations.
- **Best-supported claims:** Recent studies establish heat-related ED visits, mortality burden, unequal vulnerability, indoor exposure risk, and the existence/common components of heat plans and warnings.
- **Weakest evidence link:** The pool does not support precise local effectiveness estimates for specific interventions. Most greening and governance studies are observational, modeled, survey-based, or plan descriptive; full texts are needed before causal claims are made.
- **Reviewer blind-spot checks:** Vulnerable-population and governance evidence is stronger than long-term social-consequence evidence. Indoor cooling and building retrofits appear through older adults and intervention reviews but are not separately represented by multiple building-level studies.
- **Geographic and method balance:** U.S. and international studies are included, but no single-city U.S. pool dominates. Methods include cohort/case-crossover, surveillance, index/exposure modeling, qualitative work, rapid/systematic reviews, and scenario modeling.
- **Gaps for later phases:** Local ordinances, budget/fiscal evaluations, heat officers, building-code retrofits, worker protections, transportation/transit heat, and behavioral-outcome evaluations remain outside this 20-item pool.
- **Pipeline transparency:** UC Library Search was attempted but returned only a JavaScript shell; OpenAlex/Crossref supplied discovery and verification, so books, local gray literature, and UC holdings are underweighted.
