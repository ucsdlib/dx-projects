## Coverage summary

- **Source precision:** 17 of 18 selected sources are topically relevant to at least one confirmed-scope or reviewer lens. No source is wholly irrelevant. However, src_010 is at risk of being used as empirical evidence if it is commentary, src_015 is not sufficiently supported at metadata-only level, and src_018 is too technical for unqualified novice use.
- **Reference recall:** Low—only 3 of 20 pool items appear in the selected set: Meerow & Keith (2021), Pascal et al. (2021), and Kotharkar & Ghosh (2022). The pool’s strongest recent health-outcome, equity, resident-experience, warning-system, and community-intervention sources were omitted.
- **Confirmed-scope coverage:** The selections support “what can cities do” and “what is in heat plans,” but only weakly support the public-health burden and recent healthcare-use side of the user’s question.
- **Reviewer-lens coverage:** Governance is strong; green space and cooling are good; health outcomes and vulnerable populations are partial; lived experience is weak.
- **Missing disciplines, methods, populations, or geographies:** Missing emergency medicine/public-health surveillance, epidemiological healthcare-use studies, building/housing cooling, environmental justice targeting, warning-system evaluation, and social-science resident research. Missing methods include recent case-crossover/surveillance studies, scenario modeling of avoided health outcomes, systematic reviews of community interventions, and qualitative lived-experience research. Missing populations include older adults, pregnant people, low-income households, people with disabilities or chronic illness, outdoor workers, renters, and linguistically isolated groups. No local jurisdiction is represented, which is understandable but should be flagged for council use.
- **Overrepresented literatures or source types:** Heat action plans and governance/plan-content studies are overrepresented: src_001–src_010 and src_015 all belong to this cluster. Almost all selected items are abstract-only, and src_015 is metadata-only, so none should support precise causal or local-effectiveness claims without full-text review.

## Duplicates, substitutions, and unsupported claims

- **Within-set near-duplicates:** src_001, src_002, src_003, src_004, src_005, src_009, and src_010 form a redundant governance cluster. They are not identical, but they repeat overlapping policy-process evidence.
- **Weaker substitutes:** src_005 is a weaker substitute for src_002/src_004; src_010 is a weaker substitute for the empirical plan analyses; src_008 and src_016 are weaker substitutes for the pool’s more recent outcome and intervention sources; src_012 is a weaker substitute for the pool’s direct greening–mortality evidence.
- **Unsupported as selected:** src_015 should not be used for specific plan-component claims because the selected evidence basis is metadata-only. src_010 should not be characterized as empirical intervention research unless its article type is verified. Abstract-only sources should not be cited as evidence of precise local effects or causal effectiveness.
- **No wholly irrelevant sources found:** The selected set contains no source that should be discarded solely for being off-topic.

**Reason:** The selected sources are generally relevant and policy-facing, with no wholly irrelevant item, but the set is unbalanced toward heat-plan/governance literature and underuses the reference pool’s recent health-outcome, equity, resident-experience, warning-system, and intervention-evaluation sources. The user asked broadly what the city should do about extreme heat and public health; the current set would let a novice describe governance activity better than the actual public-health burden or the comparative merits of interventions.

**Highest-priority fixes:**

1. Trim at least three redundant governance sources, prioritizing src_002, src_004, and src_009 if retaining a compact governance core; verify src_010’s article type before treating it as research evidence.
2. Add recent health-outcome evidence from the pool, preferably Sun et al. (2021) and Vaidyanathan et al. (2024), and retain or replace src_016 with Vicedo-Cabrera et al. (2021).
3. Add at least one direct equity/vulnerable-population source from the pool, such as Manware et al. (2022), Clark et al. (2024), Williams et al. (2019), or Doherty et al. (2025).
4. Add Das et al. (2025) for community-based intervention comparisons and Sarath Chandra & Lee (2025) for warning-system design/limitations.
5. Do not use src_015 for specific plan-component claims until full text is reviewed; verify all selected sources against full text before making causal or local-effectiveness claims in the council briefing.
