## Agreement criteria and review assumptions

- **Stated criterion:** Use `asks` when an objective or research question is explicit in the quoted title, objective, methods, purpose, or goal sentence. Use the source’s wording; do not manufacture a question.
- **Inferred criterion:** Use `infers` when only title/metadata implies a source objective without an explicit objective quote. Use `raises` when the evidence identifies an issue but does not state the source’s own inquiry. Use `reviews` for review-type evidence or an explicitly stated review purpose.
- **Direct support:** Mark `supports` only when the source objective or quoted evidence directly addresses the same construct, intervention, population, governance mechanism, outcome, or evidence-limitation concern. Topical proximity, a broad policy purpose, or a mention of an adjacent topic is not enough.
- **Secondary relation:** Mark `relates_to` for plausible conceptual connection that lacks a stated construct/outcome match. Mark `none` where no defensible relation exists.
- **Evidence limits:** Abstract-only status prevents verification of methods, findings, populations, and most outcomes. A conclusion in a title is treated as source-stated framing, not as proof of a full-text finding unless the supplied quote also reports it.
- **Transferability:** Evidence from California, US cities, London, Paris, India, Toronto, Phoenix, or Miami may support a general city decision area but does not establish that the same intervention will work in the user’s city.
- **Direct-support precision assumption:** Every relation marked `supports` should remain defensible at full text or with a richer quote. If a full text shows that the direct construct/outcome match is absent, it should be downgraded.
- **Direct-support recall assumption:** The candidate relations below are intended to include all defensible direct links inferable from the packet. A richer packet or full texts could reveal additional direct links, especially for review articles.
- **Canonical labels:** The user prompt is treated as a context frame, not a canonical question, consistent with the intake transcript. Candidate question areas are auditor-derived, not taken from the generated map: `G` governance/HAP/preparedness; `H` heat-health outcomes/intervention health effects; `U` urban form/cooling interventions; `E` vulnerable populations/equity; `M` measurement, evaluation, and evidence limitations; `L` lived experience/social consequences.

## Unsupported or unconfirmed candidate relations

- **Lived experience/social consequences (`L`):** No selected source directly supports lived-experience evidence. Academic reviews or governance studies may be contextually adjacent, but they do not substitute for first-person or community-reported evidence.
- **Healthcare use:** None of the supplied quotes directly examines healthcare utilization, emergency department visits, or service demand. Mortality and heat-stress outcomes do not directly support this component.
- **Direct local applicability:** No source studies the user’s city. Analogous jurisdictions can support general decision options, not city-specific conclusions.
- **Intervention-to-health causal claims:** Only `src_008` directly evaluates a HAP against mortality. Most governance, planning, exposure, or infrastructure studies should not be treated as direct evidence that a particular intervention improves health outcomes.
- **Equity benefits of urban cooling:** Several sources concern equity or urban cooling separately. Without an explicit joint analysis, equity benefit should remain conceptual unless a richer quote establishes it.
- **Intervention effectiveness:** For `src_014` and `src_018`, cooling or heat-stress performance is not automatically direct evidence of reduced morbidity or mortality.
- **Recency:** The user requested recent research evidence. Sources range from 2017 to 2026; `src_016` (2017) and `src_008` (2018) may require a recency qualifier.
- **Metadata-only inference:** `src_015` should not support detailed plan content, findings, or evaluation quality on metadata alone.
